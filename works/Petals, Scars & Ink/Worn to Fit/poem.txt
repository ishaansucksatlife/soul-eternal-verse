By the end,

the hands no longer looked familiar.

Not because they had changed.

Because they had carried too many versions
of the same person
to the grave.

The blood never washed away.

It settled beneath the nails,
dried into the lines of the palms,

finding a place

where water

could never reach.

Invisible to everyone else.

Impossible not to notice alone.

ㅤ
No one ever asked
where it came from.

It is difficult to recognize murder

when the victim
shares the same face.

There was no single death.

It happened slowly.

A massacre measured
in smaller versions.

The curious.

The gentle.

The unbearably honest.

The part that still believed
there was a place
waiting somewhere.

Each carried out
before the next
could take their place.

The hands remember everything.

They remain stained.

Not with someone else's blood.

With their own.

With every self
they learned to slaughter

so another room

might mistake them

for someone worth keeping.

ㅤ
One by one,

they disappeared.

The loud.

The hopeful.

The part that mistook
endurance
for belonging.

The part that kept returning,

placing another chair

at tables

already set

for someone else.

None of them survived.

Each was buried

long before

anyone noticed

they had ever lived.

ㅤ
The room stayed full.

Only the mirror
began forgetting
who belonged inside it.

Some reflections arrive late.

Some never arrive at all.

There were shelves

where old selves
used to live.

Empty now.

Only outlines remained,

the way paintings
leave lighter squares
after being taken down.

The dust remembered them

longer

than anyone else did.

ㅤ
The replacements
were convincing enough.

They smiled
when expected.

Agreed

before disagreement
could become inconvenient.

Learned the architecture
of acceptance.

Wore it well.

Yet every room

required another alteration.

Another disappearance.

Another name

left folded

inside yesterday's clothes.

ㅤ
Still,

someone else
was always easier to keep.

Newer.

Lighter.

Untouched by repair.

The sort of person

who had never learned

how exhausting it is

to sand away
every rough edge,

only to be told

there is still

too much of them left.

Eventually,

there was nothing left
to remove.

Only the hands.

Still stained.

Still carrying
the quiet evidence

of everyone
they had buried

just to make room

for strangers.

ㅤ
The strange thing

was never being left behind.

It was looking back

and finding

no footprints

belonged

to the original.

The house was full.

The conversations continued.

Names changed.

Faces changed.

Laughter found
new directions.

Nothing paused.

Nothing noticed.

The walls accepted every version.

Remembered

none of them.

ㅤ
An old toy
remains on the shelf
for a while.

Not because anyone
still wants it.

Because no one

has remembered

to throw it away.

Dust gathered first.

Then silence.

Then the slow certainty

that forgetting

requires no decision.

Only time.

ㅤ
The blood remained.

Not theirs.

Never theirs.

It belonged to every version
that had been dragged
to the edge of itself,

cut away,

left behind,

so another stranger
might find something
easier to love.

ㅤ
Years later,

the hands were still stained.

Not because the wounds
never closed,

but because there was no one left
inside them

worth washing them for.
