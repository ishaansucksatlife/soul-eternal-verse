There is a particular kind of discomfort
that arrives when love
is handed back to you
in pieces.
‎‎‎‎‎‎‎‎ㅤ
A little attention.
A little affection.
A little time
when nothing better
is asking for it.
‎‎‎‎‎‎‎‎ㅤ
They give it names now.
Situationships.
Microships.
Breadcrumbing.
Almosts.
Whatever new word
makes uncertainty
sound deliberate.
‎‎‎‎‎‎‎‎ㅤ
I cannot make myself
believe in any of them.
‎‎‎‎‎‎‎‎ㅤ
Not because I need
some perfect definition
of what two people should be,
but because I have never understood
how something as intimate as love
became something
people are supposed to keep
deliberately unfinished.
‎‎‎‎‎‎‎‎ㅤ
Love, to me,
has never been a placeholder.
It is not something
to keep warm
until something better arrives.
It is not a door
left slightly open
just in case.
‎‎‎‎‎‎‎‎ㅤ
If I let someone close,
I let them matter.
‎‎‎‎‎‎‎‎ㅤ
I remember the little things.
The words they said once
and probably forgot.
The songs they played.
The way their voice changed
when they were tired.
The things they feared
but pretended not to.
‎‎‎‎‎‎‎‎ㅤ
I make room.
Real room.
The kind that rearranges
the furniture of a life.
‎‎‎‎‎‎‎‎ㅤ
I do not know how
to love someone halfway
without eventually
standing in the ruins
of everything I withheld.
‎‎‎‎‎‎‎‎ㅤ
And perhaps that is where
the dysphoria begins.
‎‎‎‎‎‎‎‎ㅤ
The feeling of inhabiting
a version of love
that does not feel like mine.
‎‎‎‎‎‎‎‎ㅤ
I watch people move through each other
without arriving anywhere.
They hold hands
without holding responsibility.
They say they care
without deciding what care means.
They disappear for days
and return casually,
as though absence
were only another form of affection.
‎‎‎‎‎‎‎‎ㅤ
Everyone seems to understand
the rules except me.
‎‎‎‎‎‎‎‎ㅤ
So I wonder
if I am doing it wrong.
‎‎‎‎‎‎‎‎ㅤ
If I am too serious
for something everyone else
has learned to treat casually.
If wanting consistency
is somehow demanding.
If wanting honesty
is asking for too much.
If calling something love
means I am supposed
to accept all the ways
people have learned
to avoid loving properly.
‎‎‎‎‎‎‎‎ㅤ
Sometimes I try.
‎‎‎‎‎‎‎‎ㅤ
I tell myself
not to care so deeply.
Not to expect too much.
Not to attach meaning
to every kindness.
Not to imagine a future
inside every beautiful moment.
‎‎‎‎‎‎‎‎ㅤ
I try to become casual.
‎‎‎‎‎‎‎‎ㅤ
And it feels like wearing
someone else's skin.
‎‎‎‎‎‎‎‎ㅤ
Every instinct in me
recognizes the lie.
‎‎‎‎‎‎‎‎ㅤ
Because I do not want
a person for their availability.
I want them for their presence.
I want to know them
beyond convenience.
I want the difficult conversations,
the ordinary mornings,
the ugly days,
the choosing that happens
when there is nothing romantic
left to perform.
‎‎‎‎‎‎‎‎ㅤ
I want love to mean
that two people
have decided to be real
with each other.
‎‎‎‎‎‎‎‎ㅤ
Not permanently perfect.
Not endlessly certain.
Just honest enough
to stop treating each other
like temporary arrangements.
‎‎‎‎‎‎‎‎ㅤ
And still,
I find myself wondering
whether refusing all of this
means I am refusing
the way the world loves now.
‎‎‎‎‎‎‎‎ㅤ
Maybe I am.
‎‎‎‎‎‎‎‎ㅤ
Maybe there is something
wrong with me
for wanting something
that does not need
a clever name
to justify its existence.
‎‎‎‎‎‎‎‎ㅤ
But there is another fear
beneath that one.
‎‎‎‎‎‎‎‎ㅤ
That if I force myself
to become comfortable
with half love,
I will eventually
become uncomfortable
with my own definition
of love.
‎‎‎‎‎‎‎‎ㅤ
And that frightens me more.
‎‎‎‎‎‎‎‎ㅤ
Because I can survive
someone misunderstanding
how I love.
‎‎‎‎‎‎‎‎ㅤ
I do not know
if I can survive
becoming someone
who no longer recognizes it.
‎‎‎‎‎‎‎‎ㅤ
So perhaps this dysphoria
is not the feeling
of being incapable of love.
‎‎‎‎‎‎‎‎ㅤ
Perhaps it is the feeling
of knowing exactly
what love means to me,
then watching myself
try to betray that meaning
just to belong
inside a version of it
everyone else seems
to understand.
‎‎‎‎‎‎‎‎ㅤ
I would rather be lonely
with an intact definition of love
than loved
in a way that makes me
feel like I have abandoned myself.
