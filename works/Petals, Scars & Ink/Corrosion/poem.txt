Love does not end
when people finally leave.
By then,
the damage has already happened.

The room smells different.
The sheets hold the smell of two people
who stopped reaching for each other.
The coffee tastes burnt

because no one remembers
how the other used to drink it.

The silence has been sitting there
for so long
it has become part of the furniture.

It starts with small things.
A sentence swallowed.
A hand pulled back.
A wound left uncovered

because explaining it
feels worse than carrying it.

Someone stops saying,
"That hurt."
Not because it doesn't.
Because the words taste bitter

after being ignored too many times.
The mouth learns
what it is safer not to say.

The body learns
what it is safer not to ask for.

Someone starts cutting pieces away.
Not dramatically.
Not all at once.
Just enough

to make living together easier.

A laugh becomes quieter.
A voice becomes softer.
A person becomes careful
around the person

who once felt like home.

No one tells anyone
to disappear.
They just make disappearance
the least painful option.

The other person changes too.
They stop noticing.
Not because they do not care.
Because noticing means admitting

something is wrong.
And admitting something is wrong
means touching the place

where everything is already rotting.

So they look away.
They get good at looking away.
No one screams.
No doors break.

No one becomes the kind of monster
people warn you about.
They still hold umbrellas
for each other.

Still ask if they ate.
Still say goodnight.
That is the part that hurts.
The worst things

do not always arrive loudly.
Sometimes they sit beside you
every night

and ask how your day was.

The bed gets colder.
No one says anything.
A body can feel
another body leaving

long before a person admits it.

A hand stops searching
in the dark.
A shoulder stops moving closer.
A space grows between them

wide enough
to fit every word
they never said.

One day,
someone hears cruelty
come out of their own mouth.
A voice they recognize.

A voice they hated.
A voice they swore
would never belong to them.
The other person goes quiet.

Not shocked.
Not angry.
Just tired.
Because pain repeated often enough

stops feeling like an injury.
It starts feeling normal.

Years later,
they will still remember
the small things.
The cup.

The chair.
The side of the bed.
The way someone liked their coffee.
The way someone used to touch their arm

without thinking.

The strange part is not losing a person.
The strange part is remembering
exactly when they stopped being one.

The photographs keep lying.
Two people smiling.
Two people touching.
Two people standing close enough

to fool anyone else.

But the camera cannot catch
the moment someone's eyes
stop searching for yours.
It cannot capture

the second a familiar face
starts feeling like a stranger.

The decay is quiet.
That is why nobody notices.
It smells like old rooms.
Like clothes left wet too long.

Like food forgotten
in the back of a fridge.
It tastes metallic.
It feels cold.

It sits underneath the skin
until even love starts feeling
like something the body
needs to reject.

When they finally admit it,
nothing happens.
The dishes stay in the sink.
The lights stay on.

The house stays exactly the same.
Two people stand inside it,
both knowing
they have been mourning something dead

while it was still breathing.

There was no betrayal.
No single sentence.
No moment worth blaming.
Just two people

slowly becoming unbearable to each other,
until one day
they sat across from someone

they had spent years loving,
and felt nothing
except the relief
that it was finally over.
